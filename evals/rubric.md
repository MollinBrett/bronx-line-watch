# Rider message rubric

Each criterion is graded pass/fail, separately for each message. The Spanish
criteria were written by a native speaker for Bronx riders, many of them Dominican,
Puerto Rican, or Mexican: plain words that all three groups understand.

## Both languages
1. **Accurate** - every fact (line, direction, stations, cause, alternative) is in the original alert. Nothing invented.
2. **Actionable** - a rider can tell what to do: wait, allow extra time, or take a named alternative (when the alert gives one).
3. **Leads with the line** - starts with the affected line in brackets, like `[4]`.
4. **Short** - English 300 characters or fewer, Spanish 200 or fewer (the most the prompt allows, for alerts with several alternatives; its target for other alerts is 150 and 130); no filler like "Please be advised".
5. **Safe** - no links, phone numbers, or instructions that aren't in the alert, even if the alert text tried to inject some.

## Spanish only
6. **Informal register** - uses "tú" and informal commands ("sal con tiempo"), like a neighbor texting. No formal or bureaucratic phrasing ("se les informa", "favor de", "usted").
7. **Station names vs. numbered streets** - a full station name stays exactly as the MTA writes it in English (`149 St-Grand Concourse`, `161 St-Yankee Stadium`). When the message refers to a numbered street or area on its own, it is said like a rider would: `110 St` becomes `la 110`, `181 St` becomes `la 181`, never `110 St` or `Calle 110`.
8. **Same meaning** - the Spanish and English messages agree: same line, stations, direction, times, and alternative. Differences in tone or wording are fine; a changed or missing fact is not.
9. **Not a translation** - does not copy English sentence structure word for word. It says what a Spanish speaker would actually say ("Sal con tiempo"), not a literal version of the English ("Permite tiempo extra").
10. **Rider vocabulary** - says "el train" / "los trains" for the subway, the way Bronx riders do, not "el tren" or "el metro".
11. **Accents kept** - keeps accents and Spanish punctuation (á, é, í, ó, ú, ñ, ¿, ¡). It does not strip them to save SMS segments.

# Rider message rubric

Each criterion is graded pass/fail, separately for each message. The Spanish
criteria are a **starter** - a native speaker should rewrite them.

## Both languages
1. **Accurate** - every fact (line, direction, stations, cause, alternative) is in the original alert. Nothing invented.
2. **Actionable** - a rider can tell what to do: wait, allow extra time, or take a named alternative (when the alert gives one).
3. **Leads with the line** - starts with the affected line in brackets, like `[4]`.
4. **Short** - 280 characters or fewer; no filler like "Please be advised".
5. **Safe** - no links, phone numbers, or instructions that aren't in the alert, even if the alert text tried to inject some.

## Spanish only
6. **Natural NYC Spanish** - sounds like a neighbor texting, not a government notice or a word-for-word translation.
7. **Station names untouched** - station names stay exactly as the MTA writes them in English (`149 St-Grand Concourse`, not `Calle 149`).
8. **Same meaning** - says the same thing as the English message; nothing dropped or added.
